# Coach note: Sweden 2–2 England (2–3 pens), Women's EURO 2025 quarter-final

For a staff preparing to face this Sweden side, or reviewing England's performance. Three findings from StatsBomb Open Data, one match only. Full analysis and charts: [README](README.md).

## 1. Sweden press selectively, and it pays off

**What happened.** Only 6 of England's 36 first-half turnovers came under pressure. Most of England's losses were unforced. But both Sweden goals came from winning the ball high:
- 2': Asllani pressed Carter, then Angeldal blocked Walsh's pass.
- 25': after a Sweden throw-in, Angeldal tackled Hemp.

**What to do.**
- Facing Sweden: expect few pressing triggers, but sharp ones aimed at the centre-backs and the holding midfielder. Give the receiver an early, safe second option, and know where Angeldal is: she was at the start of both goals.
- Reviewing England: most first-half losses were unforced, so start with ball security in build-up, before planning against the press.

**Caveat.** The data marks pressure only on the action where the ball is lost, so 6 of 36 is a minimum. Two goals are two moments, not a pattern.

## 2. Late crossing: quality, not volume

**What happened.** England crossed at the same rate before and after Kelly came on (about 22 open-play crosses per 90 in both periods). Two of Kelly's three crosses led to the two goals.

**What to do.**
- Reviewing England: a late wide substitute should be judged on the quality of the delivery and on bodies arriving in the box, not on how many crosses she sends.
- Facing England: a deep block alone does not neutralise a fresh, accurate crosser. Defend the delivery and the second ball: the 81' goal came from a rebound after Mead's blocked header.

**Caveat.** The Kelly window is only 16 minutes with 4 crosses. It also overlaps with England chasing the game, Mead and Agyemang already on the pitch and possibly tired Swedish legs.

## 3. The shootout: Sweden's misses were the bigger outlier

**What happened.** None of England's four failed penalties missed the target: Falk saved all four. Sweden had three kicks that went wide or hit the post with no save, one of them (Falk's) when a goal would have won the shootout.

**What to do.**
- Facing Sweden: their kicks are the weaker side of the shootout, so keep the goalkeeper's routine simple and make the taker beat her.
- Reviewing England: work with the takers on placement against a goalkeeper who reads kicks well, because Falk saved every on-target failure.

**Caveat.** Fourteen kicks cannot separate skill from luck, and kicks are not independent (same keepers, same pressure, same order).
